# FitLoop at Generating Genius — Entrepreneurship Genius (2026)

## Event and result

**Programme/competition:** Generating Genius Entrepreneurship Genius business competition  
**Pitch date:** 5 September 2026  
**Entry:** FitLoop, a team idea for connecting people who need clothing alterations with local tailors  
**Result:** The team did not win the overall competition. I received an **Outstanding Individual** award (a separate £20 prize in addition to the programme participation award, as recalled in my post-event notes).

The competition required our team to present a business idea to a judging panel and respond to questions. The project aimed to give customers an alternative to unnecessary clothing returns through a simpler local tailoring journey.

## My role

I worked on the product side: designed and built the interactive competition website, developed the demo's customer flows, arranged the domain and public deployment, and designed and presented pitch materials. I prepared key pitch sections covering the **solution/demo**, **customer acquisition** and **risks**, and participated in the final Q&A.

The rest of the business concept and pitch belonged to our team; this case study distinguishes my product and presentation contribution.

## How we approached the pitch

Our presentation covered the problem, proposed solution, customer journey, market positioning, business model, launch/acquisition strategy, operational risks, sustainability and an investment ask. The demo helped judges see how a customer could discover a tailor, submit an alteration request, compare a sample quote and track progress.

We received positive comments on the website and slides. The event also gave me opportunities to speak to people about technology careers, entrepreneurship and apprenticeships.

## What I would change

Our proposed investment valuation was improvised rather than supported by a robust financial model. I wouldn't present it as a validated valuation. If pitching again, I would prepare a clearer bottom-up estimate of customer acquisition costs, transaction economics and local tailor supply, supported by interviews or pilot data.

The prototype communicated the idea convincingly, but it did not establish demand, paying customers, production operations or product–market fit.
